# Security event reporting procedure

## What to report

Report anything that might harm the security of our information. Examples: a stranger in the production hall without a visitor badge, a lost laptop or access card, a suspicious email or link, a warning from your antivirus, an unlocked server room, a colleague asking for your password, or a supplier telling us they have had a security problem.

## How to report

The security desk is the single point of contact for all reports. Send ordinary reports by email to security@nordlicht.example or as a ticket in the IT service portal. For anything urgent, such as an active attack, a stolen device or an intruder on site, call the security hotline on extension 4400, which is staffed during working hours and forwarded to the on-call administrator at other times.

The contact details are on the intranet start page and on a poster next to every time clock.

## Your duty to report

Every employee must report security events straight away. The reporting duty is part of the employment contract and is covered in the yearly awareness training.

## Handling a report

The security desk records every report in the security event register within one working day. It sorts each report into personnel, physical or IT, decides whether it is a mere observation, a weakness or an incident, and gives it a priority of low, medium, high or critical. Critical reports are passed to the information security officer at once.

## Who handles what

Personnel events go to HR, physical events to the facility manager, and IT events to the IT operations lead. Where an event touches more than one area, the information security officer coordinates. Each owner names a deputy for when they are away.

## Feedback

The security desk tells the reporter what happened to their report within two working days.

## Learning from incidents

After every incident of high or critical priority, the information security officer holds a short review with those involved and records what we will change.
