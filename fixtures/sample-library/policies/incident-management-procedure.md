# Incident management procedure

## Purpose

This procedure sets out how the incident team handles a security event once it has the event in front of it: how the event is classified, who handles it, how quickly, and who is told.

## The incident team

The incident team is the information security officer, the IT operations lead and the head of facilities. Each has a named deputy, so the team can act when one of them is absent or on holiday. The information security officer coordinates any event that touches more than one area.

## First look

The information security officer looks at each new event within one working day and records it in the incident log with its date, a description and who is handling it.

## Classifying an event

Each event is given a category, a class and a priority.

- Category: people, physical security, IT and cyber security, or suppliers and partners.
- Class: not security relevant, an observation, a suggested improvement, a vulnerability, or a security incident.
- Priority: low, moderate, severe or critical.

The category decides who handles the event: people events go to HR with the information security officer, physical events to the head of facilities, IT and cyber events to the IT operations lead, and supplier events to purchasing with the information security officer.

## Response times

| Priority | Handling starts within | Resolved or contained within |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | 1 hour, also outside working hours | 24 hours |
| Severe | 4 working hours | 3 working days |
| Moderate | 1 working day | 10 working days |
| Low | 5 working days | 30 working days |

## Escalation

An event whose handling has not started within its response time goes to the information security officer. A critical or severe event still open after its resolution time goes to the managing directors. Critical events are always passed to the managing directors at once by phone.

## Responding

The handler contains the damage first, then removes the cause and restores normal operation. Each step is noted in the incident log.

## Suppliers' incidents

For an incident at a supplier or service provider that touches the work they do for us, the information security officer judges what it means for our own systems and data, starts our own handling for anything affected, and checks whether any of our notification duties apply.

## Notification duties

The information security officer keeps the list of duties to notify others, with their contact details: the data protection authority within 72 hours of a personal data breach, and customers as their contracts require. Where an incident may involve a crime, the managing directors decide whether to involve the police.

## Communication

Only the managing directors approve what we say about an incident outside the company. The information security officer prepares the message, using the prepared templates for customers, suppliers and staff, and agrees with legal counsel what may be said. Customers affected hear from their account manager by phone, then in writing.

## Learning from incidents

After every severe or critical incident, the incident team meets within two weeks to agree what to change. The changes are tracked as tasks until they are done, and the information security officer summarises them for the yearly management review.
