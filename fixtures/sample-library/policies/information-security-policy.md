# Information security policy

## Why security matters to us

Our customers trust us with drawings, prices and plans that they share with nobody else. Losing that trust would cost us orders, so protecting information is part of how we do business, not an extra.

## Our objectives

We keep information confidential to the people who need it, correct and complete, and available when the business needs it. Each year management sets three measurable security objectives and checks progress on them at the management review.

## Where our requirements come from

Every January the information security officer collects the security requirements that apply to us: from the business strategy, from customer contracts and their security annexes, and from the laws that bind us, such as data protection law. The list is kept on the intranet next to this policy and is updated whenever a new contract or law adds a requirement.

## Approval

The managing directors approved this version on 16 February 2026. Any change to this policy needs their approval again.

## Making the policy known

This policy and the standards below it are published on the intranet, where every employee can read them. New employees receive the policy on their first day. When a change affects staff, the information security officer sends a short summary of it to all employees; when a change affects suppliers or service providers, purchasing sends them the summary with the next order.

## Supporting standards

This policy is made concrete by these documents, each with its own owner:

- the access rights policy,
- the user account standard,
- the authentication standard,
- the awareness and training concept,
- the incident management procedure,
- the cryptography standard,
- the data transfer standard,
- the logging standard,
- the vulnerability management standard,
- the backup policy.

## If the policy is not followed

Breaking this policy or a standard below it can lead to measures under employment law, up to dismissal, and for suppliers to the end of the contract. Deliberate breaches that cause damage may also have legal consequences.

## Keeping the policies current

Each policy and standard names a review cycle. The owner reviews the document before its cycle runs out, and at least every two years, and has any change approved. The information security officer keeps a list of review dates and reminds owners a month before a review is due.

## Who to ask

The information security officer is the contact person for every question about information security. Their name and how to reach them are on the first page of the intranet.
