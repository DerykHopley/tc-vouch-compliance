# User account standard

## One person, one account

Every user has a personal account of their own. Accounts shared by several people are allowed only on shop floor terminals that show production data and change nothing, where knowing who did what is not needed; each such account is listed with its purpose.

## Creating accounts

HR asks IT for a new employee's account in a ticket at least a week before the start date. The line manager confirms the request, and IT creates the account only after both have signed off. A new account starts with the basic role every employee has, which gives email, the intranet and nothing more; further rights follow the access rights policy.

## Handing over first passwords

IT hands the first password to the new user in person, or sends it by text message to the user's work phone. The user must change it at the first login.

## Leavers

HR tells IT the leaving date as soon as it is known. IT disables the accounts at the end of the last working day, and deletes them 90 days later. Accounts of service providers are disabled the day their job is finished, as purchasing tells IT.

## Default and technical accounts

Accounts and passwords that a manufacturer sets in advance are disabled or given a new password before a system goes live. Technical accounts used by services cannot log in interactively.

## Handling passwords and other login information

- Never tell anyone your password, not even your manager or IT.
- Never write a password down or keep it unencrypted; use the company's password manager.
- Change your password at once if you think someone else may know it.
- Do not use your work password for anything private.
- Passwords are at least 12 characters long and contain letters and digits.

## Hardware tokens

Users keep their hardware token with them, never leave it in their laptop and tell IT at once if they lose it, so it can be blocked.

## Reviewing accounts

Accounts are reviewed with the access rights in the periodic access review set out in the access rights policy.
